## Reddit

**Title**  
I maintain a Windows fork of Snapline, a screenshot shelf at the top of your screen

**Post**  
I take a lot of screenshots for bug reports and class notes. The part I kept tripping over was getting back to the one I’d just taken.

Snapline hangs new screenshots from a little clothesline at the top of your Windows desktop. Hover at the top to bring it back, then copy a screenshot or drag it into the app you’re working in. It also has markup, sticky notes, and local Windows OCR.

I maintain this Windows edition of Mohammed Abu Jubara’s Snapline, which grew out of Tendedero. I worked on the Windows animations and on keeping the shelf open while you’re dragging or editing. The hide delay is adjustable.

It’s free and MIT-licensed. There’s a Windows x64 installer and a portable build. The shelf resets when the date or Windows sign-in session changes, so save anything important to a separate folder.

Source and demo: https://github.com/pasariga/snapline  
Windows downloads: https://github.com/pasariga/snapline/releases

I’d be interested to hear which apps you drag screenshots into most often.

## GitHub community

I maintain the Windows edition of Snapline, an MIT-licensed screenshot shelf. It catches recent screenshots and hangs them at the top of the desktop so you can drag, copy, or annotate them as you work. The Windows changes focus on capture and shelf animation, an adjustable hide delay that waits for interaction, and handling ordinary maximized windows. Credit goes to Mohammed Abu Jubara’s Snapline and Alejandro Buján Pampín’s Tendedero for the original workflow.

Repository: https://github.com/pasariga/snapline  
Windows downloads: https://github.com/pasariga/snapline/releases
